# AI-Driven Retail Demand Forecasting & Inventory Optimization

End-to-end data science project for **SKU-level demand forecasting, explainable machine learning, and inventory decision support** using Python, SQL, XGBoost, SHAP, and Streamlit.

## Business Problem

Retail teams must estimate future product demand accurately enough to plan replenishment. Under-forecasting can contribute to stockouts, while over-forecasting can increase unnecessary inventory. This project connects forecasting with inventory logic so the output is not only a prediction, but also a practical replenishment signal.

The system answers two questions:

1. **How much demand should be expected for each store-SKU combination?**
2. **What safety stock and reorder point are appropriate under the modeled assumptions?**

## Dataset

The project contains a reproducible synthetic retail dataset generated by [`src/generate_data.py`](src/generate_data.py).

- **Period:** 2024-01-01 to 2025-12-31
- **Calendar days:** 731
- **Stores:** 5
- **SKUs:** 20
- **Rows:** 73,100 (`731 × 5 × 20`)
- **Product categories:** Beverages, Snacks, Personal Care, Household
- **Signals:** price, promotion, holiday, region, demand and replenishment lead time

The generator uses a fixed random seed so the data and results are reproducible. Demand includes store effects, product-level base demand, yearly seasonality, weekend effects, trend, promotion impact, holiday impact, price response, and random noise.

See [`docs/DATA_DICTIONARY.md`](docs/DATA_DICTIONARY.md) for column definitions.

## Feature Engineering

Feature engineering is performed separately within each `store_id` and `sku_id` time series.

**Demand history features**
- `lag_1`
- `lag_7`
- `lag_14`
- `lag_28`
- `rolling_mean_7`
- `rolling_mean_28`

**Calendar and business features**
- day of week
- month
- ISO week number
- weekend indicator
- price
- promotion flag
- holiday flag
- replenishment lead time

The rolling statistics use `shift(1)` before calculating the rolling window, so the current day's target value is not used to predict itself.

## Modeling Strategy

The model is an `XGBRegressor` configured for nonlinear tabular regression. XGBoost is suitable here because demand depends on nonlinear interactions among historical demand, seasonality, price and promotions.

Instead of randomly splitting observations, the project performs a **chronological split**. Earlier dates form the training period and the newest dates are held out as unseen test data. This better represents the forecasting setting because future observations are not allowed into training.

### Evaluation Metrics

- **MAE:** average absolute forecast error measured in units.
- **RMSE:** similar to MAE but penalizes larger misses more strongly.
- **MAPE:** average percentage error. A denominator floor of 1 is used to prevent division-by-zero behavior for very small demand values.

## Model Explainability

## Inventory Optimization

The forecast layer is converted into inventory-planning signals using:

```text
Safety Stock = z × Forecast Demand Standard Deviation × sqrt(Lead Time)

Reorder Point = Average Daily Forecast × Lead Time + Safety Stock
```

The project uses `z = 1.65`, which approximates a **95% one-sided service level** under a normal-demand assumption.

The resulting store-SKU recommendations are saved in [`outputs/inventory_recommendations.csv`](outputs/inventory_recommendations.csv).

This is a portfolio-level analytical formulation. A production system should additionally consider forecast uncertainty by horizon, holding cost, ordering cost, supplier reliability, minimum order quantities, service-level targets and capacity constraints.
